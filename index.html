How it works

  1. You send me an email Answer a few questions about your talk, your idea and the conference you have in mind. Your answers come to me by email.
  2. We hop on a call Right away, so we can talk and be human: where you are, what you need, and how I can best match you.
  3. I match you I pair each new speaker with a mentor who fits.
  4. You work together The two of you decide how: calls, messages, feedback on a proposal, a practice run of the talk.

What a mentor can help with

  • Finding a talk idea
  • Writing a proposal for a call for papers (CFP)
  • Choosing which conferences to apply to
  • Structuring the talk and its story
  • Slides and live demos
  • Rehearsing, delivery and nerves
  • Preparing for questions from the audience

Questions

Do I need speaking experience to get a mentor?

No. If you've never given a talk, you're exactly who this is for. If you've given a few and want to get better, you're welcome too.

How do I know if I have enough experience to speak at a conference?

You don't have to be an expert on everything. You need to know more than the audience about one little thing, and tell your own story.

Does my talk have to be technical?

Not at all. Conferences accept talks about soft skills and personal stories all the time.

What if I don't know what to talk about?

Say so when you write to me. Finding an idea is one of the things a mentor can help with, and most people have several good talk ideas without realizing it.

Does it cost anything?

No. Mentors are volunteers, and getting a mentor is free.

Who can be a mentor?

Anyone who has spoken at a developer conference and wants to help someone else do it. You don't need to be a famous speaker.

What if I'd rather read advice in a book?

As an introvert, I understand. I wrote a book called Tech Talks that takes you through it one step at a time.
